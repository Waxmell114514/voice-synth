## Definitions used

1   Introduction: The Collatz sequence and the Collatz conjecture

We address a long–standing and still–open mathematical problem regarding integer se-
quences attibuted to Lothar Collatz in the 1930s (cf. [1]). We represent the non-negative
integers with N and the positive integers with N>0 . We define a sequence and a subsequence
of positive integers.

Definition 1.1 (Sequence). For n ∈ N and xn ∈ N>0 a sequence X in N>0 is an ordered
set of positive integers denoted by X = X(x0 ) = (xn )n≥0 = (x0 , x1 , . . .).

Definition 1.2 (Subsequence). For n ∈ N and xn ∈ N>0 let (xn )n≥0 be a sequence. A
subsequence of (xn )n≥0 is a sequence (xni )i≥0 with ni < ni+1 for all i ≥ 0.

    If a sequence A is a subsequence of B, then we say that B covers A and denote the
covering relation by A l B. For a sequence A the cardinality |A| is the number of elements
in A. Definitions 1.1 and 1.2 allow sequences, for example, to have infinite cardinality, to
be non-decreasing, or to contain cycles of repeating elements. If we choose to terminate a
sequence A at the first occurrence of a particular number, say f ∈ N, then we denote the
finite sequence as Af with cardinality |Af |. The smallest n such that an = f is the stopping
time of A(a0 ) and is denoted by AS . Thus, if we have a finite sequence Af then we have
that |Af (a0 )| = AS (a0 ) + 1.

12   A Collatz Conjecture Proof

We now present our last result.

Theorem 12.1. The Collatz Conjecture is true.
Proof. We have a well–ordered, countable infinite set of unique wn+1 vertices (39) that is
complete in the odds not divisible by 3. Except for the trivial cycle we also have a countable
infinite set of unique (wn+1 , wn ) acyclic edges ((11.1) and (11.2)). By Zorn’s lemma, the
well–ordering theorem or the axiom of choice (which are equivalent [12]) we can construct an
acyclic tree that spans [13] our desired vertices, i.e., all odd numbers not divisible by 3 with
root vertex 1. The existence of this tree with the covering relation W (n) l C(n) is sufficient
to complete the proof.
