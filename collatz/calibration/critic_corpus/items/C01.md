## Claim

Abstract. Let g be a map from the set of positive integers into itself

defined as follows: Let x be a positive integer. If x is odd, then g(x) =
3x + 1, and if x is even, then g(x) = x/2. The 3x + 1 conjecture, also
called the Collatz conjecture, states: For any positive integer x there
exists another positive integer m such that the m-iterate of x under the
map g is equal to 1, i.e. g m (x) = 1. We provide an elementary proof of
this conjecture using an induction process.


## Definitions used

  We denote by N the set of positive integers (the set of natural numbers)
and by I the subset of N formed by the odd natural numbers. If k ∈ I, then
3k + 1 is even. So we can write 3k + 1 = 2n k̄, where k̄ ∈ I and n ∈ N. We
define a function f : I → I by f (k) = k̄, see [4].
    Let E be the set of all k ∈ I for which there exists n ∈ N such that
f n (k) = 1. Here f n (k) denotes the nth-iterate of k under the function f .
To prove the 3x+1 conjecture is equivalent to prove that E = I. It is known
that every k ∈ I with k < 260 belongs to E, see [1].


## Proof

Proof of the 3x + 1 Conjecture. Our proof will be done by induction. More
precisely, for each k ∈ I with k ≥ 260 we assume that all odd integers less
than or equal to k − 2 are in E, and we prove that k ∈ E. Let k = 2p h − 1
with p ∈ N and h ∈ I be such an integer.
Case 1: p = 1. Since k = 2h − 1 > 1, we have 3k + 1 = 2(3h − 1) = 2q h̄ with
q ≥ 2 and h̄ not divisible by 2. Therefore, f (k) = f (2h − 1) = h̄ ≤ 3h−1

2h − 1 = k. Since f (k) < k, by the induction hypothesis f (k) ∈ E. So there
exists m ∈ N such that f m (f (k)) = 1. Hence, f m+1 (k) = 1 and k ∈ E.

Case 2: p ≥ 2 and h ≡ 0 (mod 3). Then h = 3h̄ for some h̄ ∈ I and so k =
2p h − 1 = 2p 3h̄ − 1. Let k̄ = 2p+1 h̄ − 1. Then 3k̄ + 1 = 2(2p h − 1). Therefore,
f (k̄) = 2p h − 1 = k. Since k̄ < k, by the induction hypothesis k̄ ∈ E and so
there exists m ∈ N such that f m (k̄) = 1. Hence, f m−1 (k) = 1 and k ∈ E.
